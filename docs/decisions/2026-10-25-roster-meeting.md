# Roster meeting, 25 Oct 2026 (David and Steve Thomson)

## Decided

- CROP is first a **roster for employers** in any industry. That is the key to its success.
- An employer rosters its own full-time, part-time, casual and bank staff, with day, week and month views, by department or by person, and hours per person.
- Bank staff are viewed as a list of who is available and qualified, like an allocation screen.
- **Agency fill**: an employer that can't fill a shift sends it to agencies on CROP. Agencies are ranked 1, 2, 3 and the request escalates on a timer (example: agency 2 added after 10 minutes, agency 3 after 20).
- An **agency** works from an allocation screen: all its people, available or possibly available, for any of its client facilities. It nominates who goes, or the first of its people to respond goes.
- Agency workers must meet exactly the same criteria as the employer's own staff.
- The only difference between the employer and agency modules: employers roster, agencies allocate.

## Open questions (hold until answered)

1. Can one CROP business be both an employer and an agency (CFY rosters its own office staff and supplies nurses)? Assumed yes: modules are switched on per business.
2. Clients that don't use CROP: does an agency still need a client portal for them to order shifts, as CFY has in Entire today? Assumed yes, as a light employer account.
3. Who sets escalation times: per employer, per site, or per request (urgency)? Assumed per employer, adjustable per request.
4. What may an employer see about an agency worker: name, role, compliant yes/no? Documents? Assumed no documents; the agency stays responsible for credentials.
5. When an agency nominates someone, does the employer approve, or is it booked straight away? Assumed booked straight away if they meet the criteria.
6. Charge rates between employer and agency: per agreement, per role? (Release 2, invoicing.)
