# Projects (David and Steve, 4 Nov 2026)

## The ask

CROP must also serve businesses that run their own work, not only agencies filling other people's shifts: cafés and
restaurants, aged care homes, shops, and contractors that win a contract and set up a **project** (a construction job,
say) with work areas, their own staff (office people, supervisors, workers, and bank staff when busy), equipment and
consumables. David pasted a ChatGPT write-up and said to ignore what we don't agree with, since it doesn't know what
CROP already has. Its useful core: business → project → work area → staff and resources; project membership separate
from shift allocation; one person record across projects; equipment can't be double-booked across projects; archived
projects keep their history; words configurable; extend CROP rather than build a parallel system.

## What we build

**A project is a site.** No second hierarchy. `facilities` gains `kind` (site, the default, or project) and the
project's own fields: `code` (the business's reference, unique), `description`, `starts_on`, `ends_on` (expected
completion), `status` (planned / active / on hold / completed / archived) and `manager_user_id` (the office person who
runs it). A project's client is the site's existing `client_id` (optional). Work areas are the site's areas (rename
the word to "Work area" in Settings › Words we use). Everything already keyed by site works for projects as it is:
the roster and its templates, publishing, cover rules, stock per site and transfers between sites, equipment bookings
on shifts, Needs attention, offices and live numbers by office.

- **Archived** keeps every record and takes the project out of the pickers: it *is* the site's `active = false` (a
  database check keeps the two together; switching a project off archives it, switching it on makes it active).
  **Completed** stays in the pickers so close-out shifts and stock returns can still be entered.
- **Words.** New concept code 1025 Project (`docs/CODES.md`), so a business that has both ordinary sites and projects
  can say "Projects" for the project list without renaming its site word. The construction template already renames
  the site word to Project.

**The project team** (`site_members`): a person on a site's team for a period, with an optional work area and a role
on the team (supervisor or worker). Separate from shifts: being on the team never puts anyone on a shift, and being on
a shift never puts anyone on the team. One person record across every project; a person can be on several teams.
Never deleted: the last day is `ends_on`; a mistaken entry is marked removed with a reason. Overlapping entries for
one person at one site are refused (also by the database). Audited.

- In every "who can work it" list (the roster's candidates and right-click menu, the allocation queue's best people,
  bank fill) the team comes first **within** each group (ready, warnings, not available, can't). Never a block,
  never a warning: eligibility is unchanged, and a site with no team keeps today's order. Rows carry `team`
  ({teamRole, areaName}) so screens can mark them.

**Equipment allocated to a project for a period** (`equipment_site_allocations`): one piece of equipment, one place,
from a date to a date (or until further notice), optionally a work area and a note. Audited, never deleted (ended or
marked removed).

- **Never in two places on one day**: an allocation that overlaps another of the same equipment is refused (also by
  the database, which locks the equipment row first).
- **Booking onto a shift elsewhere is a hard block** ("Excavator 2 is allocated to Smith St until Fri 20 Nov"),
  in `equipmentForShift`, so the roster, templates and copied weeks, Needs attention and changing a shift's times all
  see it, like the other equipment blocks.
- **The other direction too**: allocating equipment that is still booked on shifts at other places in those dates is
  refused with the first booking named ("Take it off those shifts first"). We chose refusing over a warning so that
  "never double-booked across projects" holds from both sides; Needs attention would otherwise fill up with blocked
  bookings. Extending an allocation is checked the same way.
- **Moving** = `POST /equipment-allocations/:id/move {facilityId, onDate}`: the current allocation ends the day before
  and a new one starts that day, in one step.

**Consumables**: no new ledger. Stock per site, receiving to a site, transfers between sites and issuing on shifts
already exist; a project is a site, so it appears wherever sites do. New: `GET /facilities/:id/items`, what a site or
project used (net of returns) with cost and charge.

**API** (office roles): `POST/PATCH /facilities` take `kind`, `code`, `description`, `startsOn`, `endsOn`, `status`,
`managerUserId`; `GET /facilities?kind=project`; `GET /projects` (status, dates, manager, client, office, team size
today, shifts this week, unfilled this week and in the next 7 days, equipment there today, work areas; archived only
with `status=archived|all`); `GET /projects/:id` (overview: today and tomorrow rostered / filled / unfilled, this week,
team, equipment with its state and dates, stock with low-stock flags, counts); team: `GET|POST /facilities/:id/team`,
`PATCH /site-members/:id`, `POST /site-members/:id/remove`, `GET /workers/:id/teams`; equipment:
`GET /facilities/:id/equipment`, `GET|POST /equipment/:id/allocations`, `POST /equipment-allocations/:id/end|move|remove`.

**Screens** (second pull request): Projects under the existing "Clients & sites" menu (no new top-level item: a
project is a site, and a business that only runs projects can rename the site word to Project so the menu reads
"Clients & projects"); a project page with Overview, Team, Work areas, Equipment and Consumables, and links that open
the Roster for that site, Allocations for its client and Live numbers for its office; a simple "New project" form.

## Pushing back on the brief

- **Supervisor**: no separate "supervisor" column on the project. Supervisors are team members with the team role
  supervisor (they are usually workers who also work shifts), and the office person who runs it is the manager.
  A project can have several supervisors that way, per work area.
- **Live numbers and the allocation queue** count client shifts only (shifts at sites with a client). A builder's
  own project with a client (the contract owner) shows there; a café or aged care home with no client doesn't. The
  project page therefore shows its own numbers (today, tomorrow, this week, unfilled next 7 days) and links to
  Allocations filtered by its client and Live numbers by its office. A per-site filter on those two screens is a
  small later change if wanted.
- **Ranking the team first** changes the order of the who-can-work lists only at sites that have a team. The queue's
  "worked here before" line stays display only.

## Not now, and why

- **Per-project access for supervisors and managers** (a supervisor sees and rosters only their projects). This
  changes who can see what, so it waits for David's approval of the design below.
- **Budgets and costs per project** (labour against a budget, equipment hire charges): with rates and timesheets in
  Release 2. Item costs per project are already there (`GET /facilities/:id/items`).
- **Multi-location projects** (one project with several addresses, e.g. a road job along a corridor or a fit-out
  across three shops). Proposal: keep each location a site (its own address, time zone, roster, stock and equipment
  allocations) and group them later under a project group (`facilities.group_id` or a small `site_groups` table) with
  a roll-up page. Nothing built now uses an assumption that would block that.
- **Advanced project set-up** (copying a project, a template of work areas, team and equipment): later, after
  businesses have used the simple form.

## Proposal for David: per-project access (needs your yes before building)

Today every office person (owner, admin, allocator) sees every site. Proposal:

1. A new office role **Supervisor** (alongside owner, admin, allocator). Owners and admins choose which sites and
   projects each supervisor (and, optionally, each allocator) works on: `member_sites` (office person ↔ site), set as
   a list like offices. Nobody limited = sees everything, as today.
2. A limited person sees only their sites everywhere: roster, templates, Needs attention, allocations, projects,
   equipment allocated to their sites, stock at their sites, and only the people on their teams or rostered at their
   sites. Enforced in the database, not only on screens: `withTenant` also sets the person's allowed sites, and the
   site-keyed tables get a second policy (`facility_id = any(app_allowed_sites())`), so a missed filter can't leak.
3. What a supervisor can do on their sites: roster (assign, offer, confirm), team, equipment allocations, issue and
   count stock, approve timesheets later. Not: settings, credentials checking, other sites, costs (unless "sees costs").
4. In the app, a supervisor gets manager mode for their sites only.

Questions: Is Supervisor a new role, or should allocators be limitable too (or both)? Should a supervisor see people's
credentials (to know who is ticketed) but not their personal details? Should a limited allocator still see the
business-wide Live numbers?
